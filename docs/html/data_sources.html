The package database and its sources

The national areas and soil shares come from a SQLite database shipped with the package, src/landcover_assignment/database/land_use_database_0.5.0.db. The version in the name is the package version that built it.

How it is built

tools/build_database.py builds the database from the files in data_sources/. Two builds give the same file.

The database records its own provenance:

  • column_metadata: for every column, its unit, meaning, source, cell reference, transformation, decision and status;

  • sources: the documents behind each source id, with a link to each (see below);

  • inventory_values: every extracted inventory value, with notation keys;

  • build_info: the package version and the SHA-256 of every input file.

The tables the code reads (forest_data, grassland_data, cropland_data, wetland_data, settlement_data, NIR, forest_soil_yield_mapping) keep their 0.4.0 names and columns. Two tables are new in 0.5.0: managed_forest_data and forest_fire_rate (cbm_runner’s managed forest and forest fire, decision D6a).

Forest area and fire: mirroring cbm_runner (D6a)

The forest row mirrors cbm_runner 0.6.1, so that landcover_lca and cbm_runner work on the same forest area and the same burnt area. cbm_runner’s historic forest disturbances are aligned with the NID and forest expert judgement: they were prepared by the forest consultant who prepared them for the NID. Every value is derived from cbm_runner’s database (cbm_runner_database_0.6.2.db, the file inside the 0.6.1 release) by tools/export_cbm_runner_forest.py, and the database records it with source CBM061 and status derived.

cbm_runner models managed forest in two streams, the same in every scenario, plus the scenario’s own afforestation:

  • FM, forest present before 1990: 420,417.2 ha at its 2016 baseline, less about 1,000 ha/yr of deforestation;

  • AF, forest planted since 1990: the planting record, with baseline planting continuing to 2070;

  • SC, the scenario’s afforestation, which is what this package’s afforestation table gives cbm_runner.

The package’s forest row is built the same way:

rule

area

FM + AF in that year (managed_forest_data.total_ha); target-year rows add the scenario’s afforestation

drained organic

the NID calibration-year hectares (287.4 kha N-poor, 97.4 kha N-rich in 2020), held fixed

mineral

the rest. The open area on organic soil and roads that CBM doesn’t model are not emitting (as in the NID), and AF planting after the calibration year is on mineral soil

burnt

FM fire + AF fire in that year (managed_forest_data.burnt_ha); target-year rows add rate x the scenario’s afforestation

The scenario fire rate is cbm_runner’s (SCDisturbances._calculate_national_fire_rate): mean annual FM fire (9,826 ha over 2017-2070 = 182.0 ha/yr) plus mean annual AF fire (6,872 ha over 2008-2070 = 109.1 ha/yr), divided by the FM and AF inventory areas (420,417.2 + 612,488.4 ha): 2.8177e-4 per year (forest_fire_rate.rate_per_year). cbm_runner plants the scenario’s afforestation evenly up to its afforestation end year and each year burns rate x the area planted so far, so in the target year it burns rate x all of it.

Before 0.5.0 the forest area was the inventory area held constant (plus the scenario’s afforestation), and the burnt area was 178 ha in every scenario and year (CRF 4.A.1 only; new forest never burned).

The transition matrix and the afforestation table do not change. Between 2020 and 2050 managed forest grows by 141,393 ha in every scenario (AF baseline planting less FM deforestation). cbm_runner plants and burns that itself, so it is not a transition from grassland: Grassland_to_Forest is the forest row’s change less the managed-forest change, which is the scenario’s afforestation. Otherwise cbm_runner would plant it a second time. For the same reason the target-year land-use table’s total is the baseline total plus the managed-forest change.

Against the inventory (2020). CRT 4.A is 798,747 ha, 68,648 ha more than CBM’s managed forest: the NID open area on organic soil (46,810 ha) and roads and other areas that CBM doesn’t model. None of it emits, so landcover_lca’s forest drainage is charged on the same NID hectares as before. CBM’s 305 ha burnt compares with CRF 2024 4.A 300 ha. The calibration year must be 2017 or later (cbm_runner’s FM fire starts in 2017).

Forest soils

The forest area splits into four parts (2020): mineral soil 367.1 kha (0.460), drained N-poor organic 287.4 kha (0.360), drained N-rich organo-mineral 97.4 kha (0.122), and open area on organic soil 46.8 kha (0.059). The open area is never drained, so nothing emits from it. These are the inventory’s (CRT 4.A) parts; the package’s forest row uses the NID drained hectares on cbm_runner’s managed forest area (above), so its shares are 0.394 / 0.133 / 0.473.

For a year that is not printed, each table’s drained N-poor and drained N-rich areas are interpolated in a straight line between the printed years either side (for example 1993 = 1990 + 3/5 of the change to 1995), and the two tables are then added. Printed years, including the calibration year 2020 and 2022-2024, are the NID values exactly. The rule is in tools/build_database.py (nid_series), and each column’s transformation entry in column_metadata repeats it.

Before 0.5.0 the drained areas were hand-typed values with no source (174.5 and 72.5 kha in 2020), and the mineral share was 1 minus the drained shares (0.68), which counted undrained organic soil as mineral.

Grassland organic soils: nutrient rich and nutrient poor

The NID charges nutrient-rich and nutrient-poor organic grassland soil at different rates but publishes no area for each. The package works out the split for each year from the inventory itself. The CRT gives, for each grassland organic row (drained, and rewet status), the CH4 emitted per hectare. That figure is a mix of the NID rich and poor factors, weighted by area, so

rich fraction = (CRT CH4 per hectare - poor factor) / (rich factor - poor factor)

The drained factors combine the field and ditch factors, with 5% of the area in ditches (NID p.271). The rewet-status factors are printed in kg CH4-C and converted to kg CH4 (x 16/12, units.CH4_C_TO_CH4). The factors are in data_sources/nid2026_grassland_organic_factors.csv, the rule in tools/build_database.py (grassland_rich_fraction), and each column’s transformation entry repeats it. In earlier years the drained land was richer (0.551 in 1990).

Checks. The rich and poor soils also lose carbon at different rates (drained 6.1 and 1.3 t C/ha, rewet status 0.5 and 0.85). With the split above, the package areas reproduce the CRT grassland organic soil carbon (Table4.C, 4.C.1) in every year, 1990-2024 (tests/test_database_build.py). The CRT N2O per hectare for drained grassland (Table3.D) gives the same drained fraction (0.4525 in 2020).

Scenarios. The future split is not projected on its own. Drained land that stays drained keeps the calibration year’s rich fraction; rewetted land carries that fraction into the rewet-status pool.

Before 0.5.0 both rows were split 0.45 / 0.55 every year, with no recorded source. landcover_lca’s inventory tests use the same rule as above.

Target-year rows burn at the 3-year rate over 2022-2024 (sum of burnt area / sum of area; NationalLandCover.get_burnt_rate, BURNT_RATE_YEARS), applied to the whole row, so land a scenario adds to wetland or cropland burns too: grassland 1.64e-4, wetland 2.61e-4, cropland 6.4e-5 per year. The catchment tree keeps its own rule (the all-years average), which now covers 1990-2024; its forest still reads forest_data.burnt_kha (CRF 2024 4.A.1 wildfires). Both are on the catchment to-do list for later.

National land area by category: the NIR table (D8)

NIR holds the area of each inventory land category (forest, cropland, grassland, managed and unmanaged wetland, settlements, other land) in every year 1990-2024: CRT 2026 Table4.1, row 20 “Final area”, in kha, taken from the same extracted rows as inventory_values (4.1.final_area.*). The categories add up to the national land area, 7,111.8 kha, in every year. Read it with Loader().national_inventory_areas() (areas in ha). The model does not use it; it is there as a quick national check and for other uses of the package. Columns and labels are as in 0.4.0. The 0.4.0 table held the CRF 2024 “Initial area” under the wrong years (its extraction numbered the workbooks in unsorted file order).

Industrial peat extraction

CRT Table4.D gives 98.3 kha of industrial peat extraction (2020) and Table4(II) gives 55.5 kha. Every per-hectare emission (on-site CO2, drainage-water DOC, CH4, N2O) is charged on the 55.5 kha. The other 42.7 kha carries only the carbon in horticultural peat, which the inventory works out from tonnage, and which landcover_lca adds from export statistics. The package uses the 4(II) area, so that carbon is not counted twice.

In scenario years, industrial and domestic peat extraction phase out to 0 in 2040 (decision D10, a modelling assumption): see peat extraction in scenarios.

The 42.7 kha is taken to be the estimated area of private (non-Bord na Mona) horticultural peat production: the NID (s.6.6.3) builds the industrial area from Bord na Mona’s area plus an area worked back from horticultural peat sales, and after Bord na Mona stopped harvesting in 2021 the extra area persists (28 kha in 2024). It is in the wetland total but in no sub-share. Why the inventory charges no per-hectare emissions on it is not explained in the NID; at the industrial rates it would be about 345 kt CO2e a year (2020).
