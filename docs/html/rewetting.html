Spared area, rewetting and the grassland row

This page explains how landcover_assignment divides the grassland spared by a scenario, and why spared grassland that is rewetted stays in the grassland row.

The rule

Rewetted organic grassland is still grassland. When a scenario rewets spared organic grassland, the area does not move to wetland. It stays in the grassland row of the land-use table, and its status changes from drained to rewet status.

This follows the national inventory:

  • CRT 2026 reports “grassland remaining grassland” (4.C.1) organic soil as drained organic soil plus “Other organic soils”, which is the NID 2026 s.6.5.2 rewet status.

  • In 2020 that is 136.562 + 203.291 = 339.854 kha, which is exactly the 4.C.1 organic area. The separate “Rewetted organic soils” row is reported as NO.

  • Rewetting therefore changes the status of land inside grassland remaining grassland. It is not a land-use conversion.

Downstream, landcover_lca charges rewet-status grassland with its grassland rewetting factors (rewetting_co2_organic_soils_in_grassland, rewetting_ch4_organic_soils_in_grassland), which differ from the wetland factors. Moving the area to the wetland row would apply the wrong factors.

How a scenario’s spared area is divided

spared grassland (from grassland_production)
├── rewetted organic soil      rewetted proportion × spared area, capped by the drained organic grassland available
│     stays in the GRASSLAND row: drained share → rewet-status share
│     logged as "rewet_grassland" in the spared-area log
└── mineral soil               the rest, capped by the mineral grassland available
      allocated by the optimiser (Pyomo, HiGHS)
      ├── forest               target: forest proportion × spared area
      ├── cropland             target: crop proportion × spared area
      └── farmable_condition   whatever is not allocated to a target

Code: LandCover.calculate_spared_area_allocation (landcover.py), LandDistribution.grassland_distribution (distribution.py). The catchment tree (geo_landcover_assignment/) follows the same rule, but it tracks organic and organo-mineral pools (share_rewetted_in_organic, share_rewetted_in_organic_mineral) instead of the drained rich/poor split.

Three consequences to keep in mind when reading the outputs:

  1. The grassland row is more than productive grassland. In 2050 it holds 1,099,417 ha. grassland_production’s grassland is 1,071,079 ha; the row adds the 28,338 ha of rewetted spared land. The spared area (2,833,798 ha) is therefore larger than the fall in the grassland row (2,805,460 ha).

  2. Grassland_to_Grassland counts mineral land leaving grassland, as a negative number (−2,805,460 here). Grassland_to_Wetland is always 0. Neither of these includes rewetting.

  3. The rewetted area is recorded in the spared-area log, as land_use = "rewet_grassland" with the area in organic_area. In the land-use table it appears only as the change in share_rewetted_rich_organic and share_rewetted_poor_organic.

Rules the tests enforce

tests/test_frozen_interface.py and tests/test_land_balance.py check, on every input set, that:

  • Grassland_to_Wetland is 0 and the wetland row does not grow;

  • the logged rewetted area equals the increase in rewet-status grassland, for scenarios where the rewetting fits within the drained organic stock;

  • total land area is conserved in every scenario, and each land use balances with the transition matrix.

Known issue (to be fixed as finding 8)

When a scenario spares more organic grassland than is still drained, the log counts land that already had rewet status as newly rewetted. In scenario 3, which spares all grassland, the log records 314,200 ha. But only the 126,357 ha still drained can change status; the other 187,843 ha already had rewet status in 2020. No area is lost (the land balance holds), but the log overstates new rewetting. An expected failure in test_frozen_interface.py marks this until it is fixed.
