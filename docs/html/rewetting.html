Spared area, rewetting and the grassland row

This page explains how landcover_assignment divides the grassland spared by a scenario, and why spared grassland that is rewetted stays in the grassland row.

The rule

Rewetted organic grassland is still grassland. When a scenario rewets spared organic grassland, the area does not move to wetland. It stays in the grassland row of the land-use table, and its status changes from drained to rewet status.

This follows the national inventory:

  • CRT 2026 reports “grassland remaining grassland” (4.C.1) organic soil as drained organic soil plus “Other organic soils”, which is the NID 2026 s.6.5.2 rewet status.

  • In 2020 that is 136.562 + 203.291 = 339.854 kha, which is exactly the 4.C.1 organic area. The separate “Rewetted organic soils” row is reported as NO.

  • Rewetting therefore changes the status of land inside grassland remaining grassland. It is not a land-use conversion.

Downstream, landcover_lca charges rewet-status grassland with its grassland rewetting factors (rewetting_co2_organic_soils_in_grassland, rewetting_ch4_organic_soils_in_grassland), which differ from the wetland factors. Moving the area to the wetland row would apply the wrong factors.

How a scenario’s spared area is divided

spared grassland (from grassland_production)
├── rewetted organic soil      the rewetting target: rewetted proportion × spared area,
│     capped by the drained organic grassland available, and NEVER MORE than the target
│     stays in the GRASSLAND row: drained share → rewet-status share
│     logged as "rewet_grassland"
├── mineral soil               the rest, capped by the mineral grassland available
│     allocated by the optimiser (Pyomo, HiGHS)
│     ├── forest               target: forest proportion × spared area
│     ├── cropland             target: crop proportion × spared area
│     └── farmable_condition   whatever is not allocated to a target (mineral soil only)
└── organic soil beyond the target   only when the mineral land runs out (e.g. all herd grassland spared)
      stays in the GRASSLAND row IN ITS CURRENT CONDITION; only the animals go
      ├── drained land stays drained           logged as "spared_drained_organic_grassland"
      └── rewet-status land stays rewet status logged as "spared_rewetted_organic_grassland"

The last branch is split in proportion to the herd’s drained land not rewetted and its rewet-status land (herd area x the calibration-year rewet-status shares); the drained part is capped by the drained land. The rewet-status part also takes the small organic area that the inventory shares put in neither pool (national 2020: about 57 ha).

Every scenario has the same six log entries, in this order: rewet_grassland, spared_drained_organic_grassland, spared_rewetted_organic_grassland (in organic_area), then forest, cropland, farmable_condition (in mineral_area). Entries that don’t apply are 0. Together they add up to the spared area.

Code: LandCover.calculate_spared_area_allocation (landcover.py), LandDistribution.grassland_distribution (distribution.py). The catchment tree (geo_landcover_assignment/) follows the same rule, but it tracks organic and organo-mineral pools (share_rewetted_in_organic, share_rewetted_in_organic_mineral) instead of the drained rich/poor split.

Farmable condition is mineral soil only

farmable_condition is spared grassland that leaves production in unchanged condition (no animals, no new land use). It only ever receives mineral soil: its rows have share_mineral = 1. Spared organic grassland that is rewetted is not farmable condition: it stays grassland with rewet status (above). Rewetted land is reported in aggregate in the grassland row, so the rewet-status shares hold the rewet-status area of the calibration year (CRT “Other organic soils”) plus the rewetted spared area.

Three consequences to keep in mind when reading the outputs:

  1. The grassland row is more than productive grassland. In 2050 it holds 1,413,048 ha: grassland_production’s grassland (1,071,079 ha), the balance (313,631 ha) and the 28,338 ha of rewetted spared land (LandCover.get_grassland_area_breakdown() reports the parts). The spared area (2,833,798 ha) is therefore larger than the fall in the grassland row (2,805,460 ha).

  2. Grassland_to_Grassland counts mineral land leaving grassland, as a negative number (−2,805,460 here). Grassland_to_Wetland is always 0. Neither of these includes rewetting.

  3. The rewetted area is recorded in the spared-area log, as land_use = "rewet_grassland" with the area in organic_area. In the land-use table it appears only as the change in share_rewetted_rich_organic and share_rewetted_poor_organic. LandCover.get_grassland_area_breakdown() reports it as rewetted_spared_ha.

Transitions by soil group

Afforestation needs the soil group of each new forest hectare (it sets the yield class). TransitionDataFetcher splits each scenario’s transitions across soil groups 1, 2 and 3, using the spared area by soil group from grassland_production: soil group 1 first, then 2, then 3; in each group cropland, then forest, then farmable condition, each capped by what is left in the group. The spared area the transition matrix does not account for is the rewetted area plus any organic land spared beyond the target; it stays grassland and is placed in soil group 3 (Grassland_Spared_Organic, get_grassland_spared_organic_soil_group_areas). Rules the tests enforce

tests/test_frozen_interface.py and tests/test_land_balance.py check, on every input set, that:

  • Grassland_to_Wetland is 0 and the wetland row does not grow;

  • total land area is conserved in every scenario, and each land use balances with the transition matrix;

  • the transitions by soil group add up to the transition matrix, and each soil group’s transitions plus its spared organic area add up to its spared area;

  • rewetting never exceeds the scenario’s target, and the log’s rewet_grassland equals the increase in rewet-status grassland in every scenario (tests/test_rewetting_target.py).
